Industrial Cybersecurity Report

IIOT UNDER
SIEGE

The industrial systems that generate electricity, purify drinking water, and run factory floors were never built with network security in mind. They were built to last decades — and they have. What changed around them is everything else: connectivity, adversarial intent, and the geopolitical calculus that now treats critical infrastructure as a legitimate target. The gap between how these systems were designed and the environment they now operate in is where the modern threat lives.

THE THREAT LANDSCAPE

RANSOMWARE
Ransomware on OT Networks
Ransomware groups have shifted focus from enterprise IT to industrial control environments, where encrypting a single PLC or HMI can bring an entire production facility to a standstill. Average operational downtime following an ICS ransomware incident extends to 23 days, with ransom demands averaging $1.9 million.
CRITICAL
HOVER TO FLIP FOR DETAILS
// DEEP DIVE
What distinguishes modern ICS ransomware from earlier variants is not merely increased sophistication but deliberate industrial targeting. EKANS was constructed to enumerate and terminate specific OT process names before initiating encryption — a capability that required its developers to understand industrial control architectures in meaningful detail. When more than 60 critical infrastructure operators reported ransom payments exceeding ten million dollars apiece in 2023, it confirmed that threat actors had accurately assessed the operational cost of extended downtime and priced their demands accordingly. Entry most commonly occurs through USB delivery into air-gapped environments or through compromised software from a trusted industrial vendor.

NATION-STATE
Nation-State APTs
State-sponsored groups such as Volt Typhoon, Sandworm, and TEMP.Veles have demonstrated the capacity to embed themselves inside critical industrial infrastructure for years, waiting for the geopolitical moment to act.
CRITICAL
HOVER TO FLIP FOR DETAILS
// DEEP DIVE
The distinction that matters most about groups like Volt Typhoon, Sandworm, and TEMP.Veles is not their technical sophistication but their patience and intent. CISA's 2024 confirmation that Volt Typhoon operated inside US critical infrastructure undetected for as long as five years signals an adversarial approach that conventional detection tools were not built to identify. TEMP.Veles crossed a different threshold with the TRITON deployment: it represented an operational decision that causing industrial casualties through safety system sabotage was an acceptable strategic objective. Across both cases, the technique of using legitimate system tools to navigate victim networks — leaving minimal forensic trace — made attribution and detection exceptionally difficult.
TRITON / TRISIS
SUPPLY CHAIN
Supply Chain Attacks
Attackers increasingly target ICS vendors and technology suppliers, embedding malicious code into firmware updates, software libraries, and hardware components before they ever reach the end customer.
CRITICAL
HOVER TO FLIP FOR DETAILS
// DEEP DIVE
Supply chain compromise is particularly effective against industrial environments because it exploits the trust that OT operators must have with their equipment vendors. A device arriving from a known manufacturer with valid firmware signatures is, by every conventional measure, a trusted asset. What the SolarWinds case established was that this trust can be subverted at the vendor level without any indicator reaching the end operator. In ICS environments, where vendors retain privileged access for maintenance and remote diagnostics, a compromised vendor relationship translates directly into persistent attacker access to physical process control — with no initial foothold required in the operator's own network.
SOLARWINDS PATTERN
DEFAULT CREDS
Default Credential Exploitation
More than 90 percent of internet-exposed IIoT devices operate with factory-default or hardcoded credentials. Search engines such as Shodan index millions of such devices every day. An attacker with basic technical knowledge can gain unauthorized access to live industrial systems in a matter of seconds.
HIGH
HOVER TO FLIP FOR DETAILS
// DEEP DIVE
The persistence of default credential vulnerabilities in industrial devices reflects a market structure problem as much as a technical one. Manufacturers faced no commercial pressure to change default credentials because their industrial customers — focused on operational reliability, not security — rarely demanded it. The result is an installed base of millions of devices whose credentials have never changed since leaving the factory, all discoverable through public scanning tools that report their locations in real time. Mirai variants continue exploiting this because it works: credentials are known, devices are findable, and operators are unlikely to notice until service has already been disrupted or a pivot into the OT network has occurred.
MIRAI VARIANT
IT / OT
IT/OT Convergence Risk
The network isolation that historically protected operational technology from internet-borne threats has effectively collapsed. As Industry 4.0 integration bridges IT and OT environments, malware originating in enterprise systems now has a direct path to programmable logic controllers and SCADA systems.
HIGH
HOVER TO FLIP FOR DETAILS
// DEEP DIVE
For most of the industrial internet's existence, the premise that OT systems were protected by physical isolation held. IT-to-OT pathways existed but were narrow and deliberately controlled. Industry 4.0 dissolved this progressively and often without formal security review of the implications. The NotPetya case made the cost visible: malware designed with no ICS capability produced roughly ten billion dollars in industrial damage because IT-OT boundaries had eroded to the point where propagation was uninhibited. The convergence has not slowed since, meaning that an IT-side compromise now routinely presents an attacker with a pathway to operational systems that no network diagram of ten years ago would have shown.
NOTPETYA EFFECT
INSIDER
Insider Threats
Personnel and contractors with authorized access to operational technology systems represent a threat category that is simultaneously the highest in potential impact and the most difficult to detect through conventional technical controls.
MEDIUM-HIGH
HOVER TO FLIP FOR DETAILS
// DEEP DIVE
Insider threats in OT environments present a detection problem that differs fundamentally from the IT equivalent. In enterprise systems, user behavior analytics can flag anomalies against established baselines. In industrial networks, operators regularly perform actions that would appear extraordinary in an IT context — taking systems offline, modifying control parameters, bypassing safety interlocks for maintenance. The baseline for legitimate behavior is wide enough that malicious activity can shelter within it. Documented cases of manufacturing employees causing intentional equipment damage share a common characteristic: the actions were operationally plausible at the moment they occurred, and were only identified as malicious in retrospect.

"EVERY DECADE OF NEGLECTED SECURITY IN INDUSTRIAL SYSTEMS IS NOW A DECADE OF INHERITED VULNERABILITY THAT DEFENDERS MUST OVERCOME FROM A STANDING START."

What is distinctive about the Industrial Internet of Things is not its scale — though at over 36 billion connected endpoints it is vast — but the gap between its origins and its current reality. The protocols that industrial networks run on today were finalized when the personal computer was a novelty. Modbus arrived in 1979; the SCADA standards that followed came through the 1980s. Both were engineered for isolated, purpose-built environments where the only people with access were the technicians standing in the room. That assumption no longer holds, and the protocols themselves have not changed.


Connecting legacy OT systems to modern networks did not make them smarter — it made them reachable. An industrial controller running twenty-year-old firmware is now as accessible to a remote attacker as any web server, but without firewalls, authentication requirements, or a security team that existed when the controller was commissioned. The result is an attack surface that grows every year as more devices are connected, while the underlying systems remain fundamentally unchanged.

Major Incidents & Turning Points

ATTACK TIMELINE

2010
Stuxnet — The First Cyber Weapon

What Stuxnet established, with unmistakable permanence, was that the boundary separating cyber conflict from physical warfare had dissolved. A worm exploiting four previously undisclosed vulnerabilities was directed at Siemens S7-315 and S7-417 controllers managing centrifuges at the Natanz enrichment facility in Iran. Rather than destroying equipment outright, it manipulated rotor speeds in patterns designed to cause mechanical failure while simultaneously feeding false normality to plant monitoring systems. The operators watching those screens had no indication that anything was wrong until the damage was done. The operation, attributed to a US-Israeli joint programme, accomplished through software what previously would have required a physical strike — and did so invisibly.

NATION-STATE ZERO-DAY × 4 NUCLEAR
2015–2016
Ukraine Power Grid Attacks (BlackEnergy / Industroyer)

The Ukrainian grid attacks of 2015 and 2016 rewrote the threat model for electrical infrastructure operators worldwide. The first event — a coordinated outage affecting approximately 225,000 people, orchestrated through the BlackEnergy malware framework by the group subsequently attributed to GRU Unit 74455 — was consequential in itself. The second was categorically more serious: Industroyer, the tool deployed the following year, was purpose-built to speak directly to power grid control protocols and issue commands to substation equipment. It came within a software defect of permanently damaging transformers whose replacement lead time is measured in months. The attack demonstrated that an adversary could now threaten grid infrastructure not as a side effect of compromise, but as the primary operational objective.

SANDWORM / GRU POWER GRID 225K AFFECTED
2017
TRITON — Targeting Safety Systems

No cyberattack documented to date has come as close to deliberately killing people as TRITON. Discovered during incident response at a petrochemical facility in the Middle East in 2017, the malware framework was built to interact with Schneider Electric Triconex Safety Instrumented Systems — the devices whose sole function is to initiate emergency shutdowns when process conditions become dangerous. The operational logic was clear: disable the safety layer first, then trigger the hazardous condition. Only an error in the malware's own code prevented the attack from reaching its intended outcome. The group behind it, subsequently identified as TEMP.Veles with links to a Russian government research institute, had apparently concluded that engineering a civilian industrial catastrophe was a legitimate strategic tool.

SAFETY SYSTEMS PETROCHEMICAL
2021
Oldsmar Water Treatment Poisoning Attempt

What stopped a chemical poisoning of 15,000 Floridians in February 2021 was not a firewall, not an intrusion detection system, and not a security policy. It was an operator who noticed a cursor moving across a screen without anyone touching the mouse. The attacker had reached Oldsmar's water treatment Human-Machine Interface through a TeamViewer installation left running on an unmonitored workstation, its credentials shared across the department. Sodium hydroxide concentrations were raised from 111 parts per million to a potentially lethal 11,100 before the change was manually reversed. Post-incident examination found no network monitoring, no VPN requirement for remote access, no multi-factor authentication, and Windows 7 running on control terminals. The facility's security posture relied entirely on the assumption that nobody would ever look for it.

WATER SUPPLY TEAMVIEWER 15K AT RISK
2021
Colonial Pipeline Ransomware

The most consequential finding from Colonial Pipeline's 2021 compromise was not the ransom figure but the mechanism of disruption: a pipeline operator shut down 5,500 miles of critical fuel infrastructure not because attackers had touched the control systems, but because they might. The DarkSide group had accessed only the IT network, gaining entry through a single VPN account lacking multi-factor authentication. The operational shutdown was a preemptive decision, made under uncertainty, that produced the largest domestic fuel supply disruption in recent US history. Panic purchasing emptied filling stations across 17 states. The FBI recovered roughly $2.3 million of the $4.4 million ransom from a seized wallet, but the more significant recovery was the lesson: perimeter boundaries between IT and OT that exist only in policy documents provide no operational protection.

DARKSIDE $4.4M RANSOM FUEL SHORTAGE
2024
Volt Typhoon — Silent Pre-Positioning

Volt Typhoon fundamentally changed how security strategists think about persistent threat actors in critical infrastructure. The group, attributed to the People's Republic of China, was not stealing data. It was not disabling systems. A joint advisory from CISA, the FBI, and allied intelligence partners in 2024 confirmed that Volt Typhoon had maintained access across energy, water, transportation, and telecommunications sectors for periods reaching five years without triggering the alert thresholds of organizations actively seeking threats. The strategic framing offered by the advisory was explicit: the objective was pre-positioning, not current exploitation — establishing the capability to cause serious disruption to US infrastructure at a moment of geopolitical choice, potentially years in the future. It represented a form of deterrence infrastructure, built quietly inside the networks of the country it was intended to threaten.

Real-World Incidents

CASE STUDIES

2021
ENERGY SECTOR
Colonial Pipeline — $4.4M Ransom & National Emergency

Few cybersecurity incidents have illustrated the distance between theoretical risk and operational consequence as clearly as Colonial Pipeline. The compromise itself was unremarkable by technical standards: a single VPN account, never enrolled in multi-factor authentication, gave the DarkSide group access to Colonial's IT environment. What followed was remarkable. Colonial's operators, unable to determine whether ransomware had crossed into operational systems, shut down pipeline infrastructure preemptively — 5,500 miles of it, supplying roughly 45 percent of East Coast fuel needs. The shutdown was a rational response to uncertainty, and it produced the largest domestic fuel supply disruption in living memory. The FBI later recovered approximately $2.3 million of the $4.4 million ransom from a seized cryptocurrency wallet, but the more durable lesson was simpler: IT-OT separation that exists as policy rather than architecture provides no operational safety margin when it is needed most.

2021
WATER TREATMENT
Oldsmar Water Plant — Lye Spike of 100×

Security professionals studying the Oldsmar incident tend to arrive at the same conclusion: the attack succeeded not because of technical sophistication but because it did not need any. TeamViewer had been installed on the water treatment facility's control workstation and was never removed. Its credentials were shared among staff. The HMI it provided access to was directly reachable from the internet with no VPN interposed. When an unauthorized user raised sodium hydroxide concentrations from 111 to 11,100 parts per million — a level that could have caused serious harm to 15,000 people — the only mechanism that prevented it from taking effect was a human being who happened to be watching the screen. Post-incident review confirmed the absence of any automated detection: no monitoring tools, no segmented network, and a control system running Windows 7 software that had been out of support for over a year.

2023
HEALTHCARE / OT
Ransomware on Hospital Building Systems

Healthcare facilities have long treated building automation systems as a facilities management concern rather than a cybersecurity one. The HVAC controllers, elevator systems, and fire suppression networks governed by BACnet do not appear on most security teams' asset inventories, and they rarely receive the scrutiny applied to servers or clinical workstations. A pattern of ransomware incidents targeting US hospital networks in 2023 exposed the consequences of this classification. Attackers accessed BACnet-connected building systems through pathways that clinical security monitoring was not configured to observe, then used those footholds to reach clinical networks, encrypt patient data, and force postponements of scheduled procedures. The incidents established that in healthcare environments, the boundary of the security perimeter must extend to every networked system in the building — not only those carrying patient data.

2022
MANUFACTURING
Toyota Supplier Attack — 14,000 Cars Lost

Toyota's 2022 production suspension posed a question that just-in-time manufacturing executives had long preferred not to examine directly: how much operational resilience exists when a single digitally connected supplier fails? The answer, in the case of Kojima Industries — a supplier of plastic components whose ransomware attack Toyota could not work around — was none. All 14 domestic manufacturing facilities suspended operations simultaneously for a full day. Approximately 13,000 vehicles were removed from the production schedule as a direct result. Toyota's own systems were untouched. The lesson was not about Toyota's security posture but about the structural fragility of supply chains in which operational continuity depends on real-time digital connectivity to suppliers who may carry considerably less security investment than their customers.

06
Hardening Your Industrial Infrastructure

DEFENSE STRATEGIES

01
SEGMENTATION
Network Segmentation

Effective network segmentation requires the physical and logical isolation of operational technology environments from enterprise IT networks through the deployment of demilitarized zones, data diodes, and restrictive firewall rule sets. Established frameworks including the Purdue Model and the ISA-95 standard provide hierarchical zoning architectures that contain lateral movement within defined network segments. The objective of segmentation is categorical: a security incident that begins in the business network must be architecturally prevented from propagating to process control systems on the shop floor.

02
MONITORING
Continuous OT Monitoring

Visibility into operational technology network activity requires purpose-built intrusion detection systems that understand industrial protocols and can distinguish anomalous behavior from normal operations. Platforms such as Dragos, Claroty, and Nozomi Networks provide passive monitoring capabilities that build a baseline of expected OT network behavior and generate alerts when deviations occur. Passive monitoring is essential in OT environments because active scanning can disrupt the timing-sensitive communications of industrial control systems, potentially causing equipment faults or process interruptions.

03
PATCHING
Risk-Based Patch Management

Patch management in operational technology environments cannot follow the same cadence applied to enterprise IT systems. Industrial equipment typically operates on maintenance windows measured in months or years, and firmware updates must be tested against production-equivalent configurations before deployment. In the interim, virtual patching via IDS signatures provides a viable protective measure. Vulnerabilities should be prioritized according to their CVSS severity score, network exposure, and the criticality of the affected system to ongoing operations.

04
ZERO TRUST
Zero Trust for OT

The zero trust security model requires that access to operational technology systems be granted on the principle of least privilege, meaning each user, service, and device receives only the permissions strictly required for its defined function. Multi-factor authentication must be enforced for all remote access sessions into OT networks; VPN-only controls have repeatedly proven insufficient in documented incidents. Shared credentials, default passwords, and long-lived access accounts must be systematically eliminated and replaced with individually attributed credentials subject to regular rotation.

05
INVENTORY
Complete Asset Inventory

Effective security for any environment begins with comprehensive and accurate knowledge of what exists within it. In IIoT environments, where devices have accumulated over decades, often without central registration, building this inventory presents a substantial challenge. Passive discovery tools including those offered by Dragos and Claroty can identify connected devices, enumerate their firmware versions, and map network communication relationships without sending any active probes that might disrupt industrial processes.

06
IEC 62443
Secure by Design (IEC 62443)

IEC 62443 is the international standard series governing cybersecurity requirements for industrial automation and control systems. Procuring organizations should embed IEC 62443 compliance requirements into vendor contracts, requiring evidence of security certification, support for secure boot, implementation of encrypted communications channels, and the provision of authenticated firmware update mechanisms. Security must be established as a contractual and procurement requirement, not treated as a feature to be retrofitted after deployment.

THE NUMBERS DON'T LIE

IBM's 2024 breach research placed the mean time to detect an operational technology compromise at approximately 200 days. That figure deserves to be held alongside a contrasting one: the timeline for physically damaging a high-voltage power transformer in a way that requires replacement, not repair, can be as short as four hours of deliberate misuse. This disparity — between how long attackers can remain hidden and how quickly they can act — is the central asymmetry that makes ICS security categorically harder than conventional enterprise defense.


There is also a category difference in what is at stake. When a financial services firm suffers a data breach, the harm is real but bounded: records are exposed, remediation is costly, trust is damaged. When an attacker compromises the control systems governing a municipal water supply or an electrical distribution network, the harm is physical and potentially irreversible. This field is not primarily about protecting data. It is about preventing outcomes that affect people who have never heard of ICS security and will never know how close they came to harm.

2025–2030 Threat Horizon

EMERGING THREATS

26
AI-Powered Attacks on IIoT

Artificial intelligence capabilities are beginning to appear in the offensive toolkits targeting industrial control systems. Machine learning models capable of analyzing SCADA protocol traffic, autonomously identifying exploitable vulnerabilities in industrial device configurations, and adapting attack strategies in real-time represent a qualitative escalation in the threat landscape. Both Dragos and Mandiant have documented instances of large language model-assisted reconnaissance activities targeting industrial communication protocols during 2024 and early 2025. As these capabilities become more widely accessible, they are expected to narrow the gap between nation-state-level ICS attack sophistication and what is achievable by less-resourced threat actors.

27
Quantum Decryption — "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later"

Nation-state intelligence agencies and well-resourced threat actors are engaged in the systematic collection of encrypted IIoT network traffic today, with the intention of decrypting it once quantum computing capabilities mature to a sufficient level, a timeframe widely estimated to fall within the next decade. The consequence for industrial environments is significant: encrypted communications captured now, including control system configurations, infrastructure network topologies, and operational parameters, may eventually be made legible to adversaries. The migration of operational technology cryptographic systems to post-quantum algorithms has barely commenced, and the long service life of ICS hardware means that equipment purchased today may still be deployed when quantum decryption becomes operationally feasible.

26
5G IIoT Attack Surface Explosion

The industrial adoption of 5G connectivity is enabling IIoT deployments at a scale previously impractical with wired or Wi-Fi infrastructure. Industrial sensors, autonomous mobile equipment, and process monitoring devices are being connected to 5G networks at pace, frequently with minimal security architecture review applied to the specific risks of wireless ICS connectivity. Misconfiguration of 5G network slicing, the technology that creates isolated virtual network segments on shared physical infrastructure, could enable lateral movement between environments intended to remain isolated, potentially exposing an entire smart manufacturing floor through a single vulnerable network slice.

28
Digital Twin Manipulation

Digital twin technology, the creation of continuously synchronized virtual replicas of physical industrial systems, is increasingly used for process monitoring, predictive maintenance, and operational planning. As operators come to rely on these models for real-time situational awareness, the integrity of the data feeding the twin becomes a security concern in its own right. An attacker who can manipulate the sensor data reported to a digital twin without altering the underlying physical process can cause operators to make decisions based on a falsified representation of reality, masking developing equipment failures, concealing ongoing physical sabotage, or triggering unnecessary emergency shutdowns that interrupt production.
